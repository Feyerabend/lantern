# Preface

Have you always longed for a Lisp Machine of your own? I did, for a long
time.

They were remarkable computers. The whole system was written in Lisp, from
the window system to the editor and the debugger, and all of it was open to
you: you could look at any part, change it, and see the change at once,
while the machine kept running. But they were expensive. They lived in
laboratories and companies, not at home, and by the time computers became
cheap the Lisp Machines were gone.

Now you can have your own. Lantern runs in a browser tab, or from a single
file opened from your disk. It boots in a moment into a world of windows,
Listeners, an editor, an Inspector and a debugger. Everything on the screen
is drawn by Lisp, and every part of it can be read and changed while it
runs. Save the world, and it is there when you come back.

Lantern is not a copy of any of the old machines. It has no microcode of
their kind and runs none of their software. It follows their ideas instead:
one language all the way down, a system you can change from inside, and
tools that know the objects they show.

The first chapter describes Lantern: what you see, how it is built, and the
language it speaks. The second tells the story of the Lisp Machines, and
compares each part of Lantern with the original it follows.
